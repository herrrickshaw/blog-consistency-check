India's animal-protein export complex is three businesses wearing one label

Buffalo meat, the rendering-industry fat it throws off, and farmed shrimp look like unrelated export categories. They share the same underlying story: India is one of the world's largest animal-protein producers, and each byproduct stream is at a different stage of being turned into an export or energy business.

The meat-export industry: concentrated in a handful of states

India's buffalo-meat (carabeef) trade is the largest of the three businesses by export value. It runs through a defined, regulated pipeline: APEDA and the Export Inspection Council jointly register and certify facilities in three categories — Category A (integrated abattoirs-cum-meat-processing plants), Category B (meat processing plants), and Category C (stand-alone abattoirs). Roughly 513 plants across the country carry this export registration, current as of APEDA's most recent published list (July 2026).

Production is concentrated in a narrow band of states. By share of India's overall meat production, West Bengal (12.62%), Uttar Pradesh (12.29%), Maharashtra (11.28%), Telangana (10.85%) and Andhra Pradesh (10.41%) together account for well over half the national total. Buffalo population itself skews even more heavily toward the Hindi-belt and west — Uttar Pradesh (30.0% of India's buffalo population), Rajasthan (12.5%), Gujarat (9.6%) and Madhya Pradesh (9.4%) — which is why UP alone hosts the largest cluster of export-registered processing capacity in the country.

The byproduct nobody has fully built out: tallow-to-biodiesel

Every abattoir and rendering operation in that 513-plant network throws off animal fat — tallow — as a byproduct. India's National Biofuel Policy already permits tallow as a biodiesel feedstock, and the country carries a B20 blending target (20% biodiesel in diesel) for 2025-26. In practice, tallow supplies only a small fraction of what actually gets used: used cooking oil (UCO) accounted for roughly 35% of India's biodiesel feedstock in 2023, while animal fat and tallow contributed only about 3%, with volumes forecast around 9,000 tonnes as of 2022.

The constraint isn't feedstock availability — it's structure. Tallow supply is almost entirely a derivative of the meat-processing and rendering industry, which means it inherits that industry's volatility. When COVID-era lockdowns cut meat exports and shut abattoirs, India's nascent tallow-biodiesel supply chain lost its raw material at the same time, a linkage flagged directly in contemporary trade reporting. A rendering sector this concentrated in a few states, feeding a biodiesel programme with an explicit national blending target, is exactly the kind of byproduct-to-energy pipeline that gets built out once the economics and collection logistics catch up with the policy mandate — it just hasn't happened at scale yet.

The other protein-export story: farmed shrimp

While buffalo meat's growth has been steady, India's aquaculture exports just had their best year on record. Seafood exports hit an all-time high of ₹72,325.82 crore (~$8.28 billion) in FY2025-26, on volumes of 19.32 lakh metric tonnes — and frozen shrimp did the heavy lifting, contributing ₹47,973.13 crore (~$5.51 billion), over two-thirds of total earnings, with shipments up 4.6% in volume and 6.35% in value year-on-year.

The forward projection is aggressive: India's shrimp market is expected to grow at a 9.60% CAGR from 2026-2035, reaching roughly 2.55 million tonnes of volume by the end of that window — a scale-up that dwarfs the tallow-biodiesel opportunity in absolute size, even though both start from the same underlying idea of turning an animal-protein production base into a structured export or energy business.

The pattern across all three: India already has the raw production base — buffalo herds, rendering byproducts, brackish-water shrimp ponds. What separates the businesses that have scaled (buffalo meat, and now shrimp) from the one that hasn't (tallow biodiesel) is regulatory and logistics infrastructure: export certification pipelines and category-based plant registration for meat; MPEDA-coordinated aquaculture extension and processing standards for shrimp; and, for tallow, still mostly ad hoc rendering-to-collection logistics without an equivalent structured buildout.
